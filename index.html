📖 Lesson 1 — How to Read a Tab

Guitar tablature (“tab”) is the easiest way to read music for guitar. No sheet music required — if you can count to 22, you can read tab.

The six lines are your six strings

A tab has 6 horizontal lines. Careful — it looks upside down at first! The top line is your thinnest string (high e), and the bottom line is the thickest string (low E) — exactly what you see when you lean over and look down at your own guitar.

e |—————————————   ← thinnest string (closest to the floor)
B |—————————————
G |—————————————
D |—————————————
A |—————————————
E |—————————————   ← thickest string (closest to your face)

👇 Try it! This is a virtual fretboard laid out the same way as a tab. Click the 0 column to hear each open string — then explore the frets.

The numbers are frets, not fingers

A number on a line means: press that fret on that string, then pick it. A 0 means play the string open (no fingers). Read left to right, like a book.

e |——0——2——3——|      play the high e string:
B |———————————|      open, then 2nd fret, then 3rd fret
G |———————————|

Here's a mini melody using just the high e string. Press play and watch the highlight move through the tab — that's the order you play the notes in. Click any number to hear just that note.

Stacked numbers = play together

When numbers are stacked in the same column, you play those strings at the same time — that's a chord. Numbers one after another are played one at a time. That's basically all of tab!

e |——————0——|
B |——————1——|   ← three stacked numbers:
G |——————0——|     strum them together (a C major chord!)
D |——2———2——|
A |——3———3——|   ← two separate numbers first:
E |—————————|     play them one at a time

📖 Lesson 1 — How to Read a Chord Chart

    
A chord is several notes played at once — the full, rich sound behind
    every song you've ever sung along to. Chord charts show you exactly where your fingers go.


    

      
The chart is a photo of your fretboard

      
Imagine standing your guitar upright and looking at the front of the neck — that's a chord chart.
      Heads up: it's turned 90° from tab!

      
    
        
  • 🪵 Vertical lines are strings — thickest (low E) on the left, thinnest (high e) on the right
    • 
        
  • ➖ Horizontal lines are frets; the thick bar at the top is the nut
    • 
        
  • 🟡 Dots show where to press. The number inside is which finger:
            1 = index, 2 = middle, 3 = ring, 4 = pinky
            (unlike tab, where numbers mean frets!)
    • 
        
  • ○ above a string = play it open  ·  ✕ = don't play that string
    • 
      

      
👇 Try it! This is E minor — the easiest chord on the guitar, and it sounds
      moody and huge. Tap the chart to strum it.

      

    


    

      
Meet your first three chords

      
These three shapes already unlock real songs. Strum each one and listen to its character:
      minor chords (Em, Am) sound moody, major chords (D) sound bright and happy.

      

      
💡 Press your fingertips down just behind the fret wire, and keep them curled so they don't
      touch the neighbouring strings.

    


    

      
The full beginner toolbox — all 10 chords

      
Here is every chord you'll be quizzed on in Lesson 2 (and every chord used in the songs later).
      Don't memorize them as 10 strangers — learn them as families. Tap each chart to strum it
      and listen for the family resemblance.


      
😊↔😢 The major/minor twins one finger apart

      
Each pair differs by a single finger. Strum E then Em, and hear one note drag the mood
      from bright to brooding. Same trick for A/Am and D/Dm.

      


      
🏕️ The big campfire chords

      
C and G are the backbone of thousands of songs. They stretch your fingers across more frets —
      go slow, and let each string ring by keeping your fingers on their tips.

      


      
🎷 The bluesy sevens easiest of all

      
E7 and A7 are just E and A with a finger lifted off — two fingers each! That gap adds the
      restless, jazzy buzz you hear in every blues song.

      


      
🧠 Memory tip: quiz yourself right here — look at a chart, say its name out loud,
      then tap to check by ear. When you can name all 10, Lesson 2 will be easy.

🤘 Lesson 3 — Your First Chord Changes

    
Playing one chord is easy. Changing between chords without stopping —
    that's the real skill. Loop these pairs slowly and keep the strums even, even if a change squeaks.


    

      
Change 1 · Em ↔ Am the shape shifter

      
Your two fingers keep the exact same shape — they just hop over one string set, and your index
      finger joins in for Am. Watch the chord names above the tab as it plays.

      

      

    


    

      
Change 2 · Am ↔ C one finger moves

      
The secret handshake of guitar: between Am and C, fingers 1 and 2 stay put.
      Only your ring finger moves. Keep the anchored fingers pressed down the whole time.

      

      

    


    

      
Change 3 · G ↔ D the campfire change

      
The most-played chord change in history. It's a bigger jump, so start extra slow —
      aim your ring finger first and let the others fall in around it.
