📖 Lesson 1 — How to Read a Tab

Guitar tablature (“tab”) is the easiest way to read music for guitar. No sheet music required — if you can count to 22, you can read tab.

The six lines are your six strings

A tab has 6 horizontal lines. Careful — it looks upside down at first! The top line is your thinnest string (high e), and the bottom line is the thickest string (low E) — exactly what you see when you lean over and look down at your own guitar.

e |—————————————   ← thinnest string (closest to the floor)
B |—————————————
G |—————————————
D |—————————————
A |—————————————
E |—————————————   ← thickest string (closest to your face)

👇 Try it! This is a virtual fretboard laid out the same way as a tab. Click the 0 column to hear each open string — then explore the frets.

The numbers are frets, not fingers

A number on a line means: press that fret on that string, then pick it. A 0 means play the string open (no fingers). Read left to right, like a book.

e |——0——2——3——|      play the high e string:
B |———————————|      open, then 2nd fret, then 3rd fret
G |———————————|

Here's a mini melody using just the high e string. Press play and watch the highlight move through the tab — that's the order you play the notes in. Click any number to hear just that note.

Stacked numbers = play together

When numbers are stacked in the same column, you play those strings at the same time — that's a chord. Numbers one after another are played one at a time. That's basically all of tab!

e |——————0——|
B |——————1——|   ← three stacked numbers:
G |——————0——|     strum them together (a C major chord!)
D |——2———2——|
A |——3———3——|   ← two separate numbers first:
E |—————————|     play them one at a time

🤘 Lesson 3 — Your First Riffs

    
A riff is a short, repeatable phrase — the LEGO brick of every song.
    Learn each one slowly, loop it until it's boring, then speed it up.


    

      
Riff 1 · “Thunder Steps” one string only

      
Everything happens on the low E string (bottom line). Use the
      Loop switch and drag the tempo down to 60–70% until your fingers know the way.

      

    


    

      
Riff 2 · “The Staircase” E minor pentatonic

      
This walks up the most famous scale in rock — up one bar, back down the next.
      Notice how the tab moves from the bottom line (thick strings) toward the top (thin strings) as the notes go higher.

      

    


    

      
Riff 3 · “Boogie Bar” two notes at once

      
Stacked numbers! Hold the low E open and rock your finger between frets 2 and 4 on the A string.
      This exact move powers about a million blues and rock'n'roll songs.

      

    


    

      
    

  


  
  

    
🔗 Lesson 4 — Link It Up

    
Songs are just riffs glued together without stopping the beat.
    The secret: while your pick plays the last note of riff A, your eyes and fretting hand
    are already moving to the first note of riff B.


    

      
Step 1 · Riff A, on its own

      

    

    

      
Step 2 · Riff B, on its own

      
Same shape as riff A, moved to the A string — so the transition is just a small hop of your hand.

      

    

    

      
Step 3 · A + B, seamlessly

      
Now play them back-to-back. Turn on Loop and keep going around: A → B → A → B…
      If you stumble at the seam, slow down — a clean slow loop beats a sloppy fast one, every time.
